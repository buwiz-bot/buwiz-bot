RICH MODE. Instead of one solid colour, each candidate is a rich avatar: a 3D head (the shell) with flat colour layers painted on its front, which the engine wraps around the head as it turns, and two eyes the engine draws where the recipe places them.

Each candidate:
- shape: "custom"; outline: the head silhouette, including hair, ears, horns or headgear, under the outline rules above.
- color: the shell colour, the subject's main colour (its hair, helmet or fur). Any saturated colour; #FFFFFF is not special here.
- layers: 1 to 8 painted regions, back to front, each {"path": "M … Z", "fill": "#RRGGBB", "opacity": 0..1 (optional), "over": true (optional: drawn above the eyes)}, in the same 0 0 100 100 box as the outline (y down) and inside it. Same path rules as the outline (M/L/C/Q/Z, closed), but they need not be star-shaped.
  - The first layer is usually the face: a large, rounded, cream-white (#F5EBD6-ish) patch on the front of the head below the hairline, as in a minimalist "Bot" avatar.
  - Then what makes the subject recognisable on the front: a hair fringe or hairline over the face, a mask band, markings, a muzzle, stripes, a headband, a visor plate.
  - Optional: a very faint oval blush on each cheek (opacity 0.4-0.6).
- eyes: where the eyes go and how they look: {"c": "#RRGGBB", "at": [x, y], "gap": g, "s": k, "rim": "#RRGGBB", "rw": t}.
  - c: the eyes' colour, the subject's iris or representative colour.
  - at: the point midway between the two eyes, in the 0 0 100 100 box (y down), inside the outline: usually on the face patch, a little above its middle.
  - gap: the distance between the two eye centres (box units, typically 14-26). s: an eye size multiplier, 0.6 to 2.2 (1 is the usual size).
  - shut (optional): "l" or "r", that eye drawn closed.
  - rim (optional): a ring around each eye that follows it as it blinks and moves, and rw its thickness as a fraction of the eye width (0 to 1.2). A mask lens, goggles or a visor ring is the eye rim.
  - A plain "#RRGGBB" is accepted too: the engine then places and sizes the eyes itself.
- To shut one eye (a wink, or an eye scarred shut), set eyes "shut": "l" or "r" (the viewer's left or right). Hair or an eyepatch over an eye is a layer with "over": true, which is drawn above the eyes; glasses too. Don't fake either with skin-coloured patches.
- Never paint eyes, eye lenses or pupils as layers: the engine draws the eyes, and a ring around them is the rim (an eyepatch is an "over" layer, above). A layer around the eyes (a mask band, a visor plate) must be placed so the eyes at at/gap/s sit inside it.
- No mouth, no nose, no text, no outlines or strokes, no tiny details: each layer is a bold flat shape that reads at 32 pixels.

Hair and headgear:
- Hair has volume in the outline itself: it flows past the jaw, or forms a fringe, buns or spikes. A bald circle with a painted hairline does not read as hair.
- The face patch is smaller than the head, so the hair (the shell colour) frames it on the sides and top.
- Headgear that encloses the head (a full helmet, a hood, a mascot suit) is the shell: draw the head with it as the outline, and the face as an opening layer in it. A hat that sits on the head in another colour is a part (below).
- Keep the face patch, and anything drawn on it, clear of where the eyes sit: no fringe, stripe or marking over the eyes, unless it is meant to cover one (then it is an "over" layer).

Parts (optional, up to 6): pieces of their own beside the head, each its own 3D volume: {"outline": "M … Z", "color": "#RRGGBB", "layers": [...] (optional), "z": -1..1 (optional)}.
- Use a part for anything that is a different colour volume from the head: a hat or helmet sitting on the head, a brim, antlers, horns, a big mass of hair in another colour, a floating cloud or symbol.
- The head stays the main outline, with the face layers and the eyes. A part's outline and layers follow the same rules, in the same 0 0 100 100 box; a part may be small (a brim, an antler) and need not touch the head.
- z: -1 behind the head, 1 in front of it (default 0, in front). A hat or brim over the forehead is in front; hair falling behind the shoulders is behind.
- d (optional, 0.05..1.6): a part's half-depth as a multiple of the head's. Hair masses use z below 0 (behind the head) and d about 1.1, so the hair wraps the head and shows at side angles.
- Examples: Luffy is a black-hair head (with the face layer) plus a straw-hat part, yellow with a red band layer, in front. Chopper is a tan head plus a pink hat part with a white cross layer, plus two brown antler parts behind the hat.
